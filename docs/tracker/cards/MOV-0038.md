## Why this exists

Photographs are what Moving's inventory workflow is made of. A person walks a
room with a phone, and the value of handing that move to their chosen AI is
that the AI can look at what is in the picture — read a model number, judge a
condition, tell a lamp from a floor vase. If the photos do not arrive, the
connection is a text database with extra steps.

Two things stop photos arriving, and neither is a permission problem. Real
photos are big: an unbounded batch of `full` derivatives can exceed the result
ceiling and fail the whole call, so an AI that asks for eight pictures gets
none rather than six. And a photo that cannot be delivered has been vanishing
silently, so an AI staring at an inventory could not tell "this belonging has
no photo" from "the photo is still processing" from "you asked for too much".
Both failures look identical to a model, and both look like the product simply
does not work.

## Current truth

Byte delivery itself is **real and shipped**, not stubbed — the earlier record
of a "stubbed delivery fetch" described the test harness, not the source.
`get_evidence_media` resolves the person's grant and move scope, then calls
`mcpToolsImages.getImages`, which authorizes through the subject bridge,
obtains a display URL from `photos.getDisplayUrlForSubject` — the same
Cloudflare Images or Backblaze B2 signed-URL path the web app uses — fetches
the bytes **server-side**, and returns them as native inline MCP image blocks.
No storage URL crosses the boundary. Nothing new is needed from the bucket, and
no new credential or environment variable is introduced.

What was missing was everything around the bytes: no per-image ceiling, no
whole-batch ceiling, no step down to a smaller size, and a bare
`catch { viewable = false }` that turned every distinct failure into the same
silence.

Delivery is now budgeted and every omission is named. Up to eight photos per
call, 1.5 MB per image and 4 MB per batch of raw bytes, chosen to stay inside
the result ceiling once base64 adds a third. A photo too large at the requested
size steps down the ladder — full, detail, card, thumb — and is sent smaller
rather than dropped. Anything still left out appears under `skipped` with a
machine-readable reason and a plain sentence: `derivative_not_ready` (ask again
shortly — the common case straight after a capture), `too_large`,
`budget_exhausted` (narrow the filter or ask for a smaller variant),
`delivery_unavailable`, `not_an_image`, `fetch_failed`. When several sizes
fail, the reason reported is the most actionable one rather than the last one.
No reason names a bucket, key, provider, or URL.

The budgets are server constants, never tool arguments. A connected AI cannot
raise the product's own ceiling.

## Next safe action

Prove it against the real bucket in the live run. Step 6 of the real-client
lifecycle in `docs/planning/moving-bring-your-ai-provider-actions.md` is where
a photo has to appear inside an actual AI product; that is the only remaining
unknown, and it is a deployment-and-client step, not a repository one.

## Constraints

- Reuse the product's existing server-side object read. Do not invent bucket
  credentials, a new delivery route, or a second storage pattern.
- Never return a storage URL, signed or otherwise, under any scope.
- Keep the permission shape untouched: grant, then move scope, then
  attachment scope. Evidence is reachable because it is attached to the work.
- Budgets are server-enforced. Do not expose them as arguments.
- A skip reason is written for a model to act on and must not leak storage,
  provider, or configuration detail.

## Completion evidence

- `convex/lib/mcpEvidenceMedia.ts` holds the ceilings, the size ladder, the
  reason vocabulary, the reason precedence, and the plain-language sentences.
- `convex/mcpToolsImages.ts` delivers within the budget, steps down before
  dropping, and reports `images` and `skipped` with byte accounting.
- `tests/unit/mcp-evidence-media-delivery.test.ts` (11 tests) proves inline
  bytes with no storage link, step-down under the per-image ceiling, the batch
  ceiling stopping cleanly instead of failing the call, still-processing and
  undeliverable photos surfacing as reasons, and the server image ceiling
  holding against a caller asking for 100.
- The lifecycle harness step 4 now asserts byte accounting on a realistic
  payload, and its honesty note says exactly what the stub replaces.
- `get_evidence_media` and `get_images` descriptions, `public/ai.txt`,
  `public/llms.txt`, `public/llms-full.txt`, and `/mcp/guide` state the budget
  and the skip contract.

## Unknown or access limitation

The storage host's own availability is not proved here: the tests stub the HTTP
GET while keeping the product's real URL construction underneath it. Only the
live run with a real AI product proves the bucket end to end.

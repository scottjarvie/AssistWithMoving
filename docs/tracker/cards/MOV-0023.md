## Why this exists

A person may direct their own AI however they choose outside Moving. Moving
still needs a precise boundary for whether that AI may use Moving's tools,
receive private move data, change a record, publish/share/export through the
product, or receive an approved external-action handoff. Without one durable
authority contract, Queue intent can be mistaken for blanket permission and a
handoff can become unattributable.

## Current truth

Moving has scoped API keys, OAuth/client foundations, Queue claims and
delegation, sensitive-field rules, private photo defaults, share-link action
permissions, revocation paths, audit logs and selected actor fields. Cards
`MOV-0008`, `MOV-0011`, and `MOV-0017` already preserve the Queue, access and
provenance portions of this problem. The Project Philosophy correctly labels
those as partial foundations. No verified product-wide contract currently
binds actor/AI, scope, permitted data, product operation or external-action
category, owner approval, expiry/revocation, and attributable handoff result in
one enforceable model.

**Priority:** Next. **Change class:** product authority, data boundary,
audit-contract, and verification gap.

## Next safe action

Define the smallest shared authority and receipt contract across the existing
OAuth, API-key, Queue, sharing and audit foundations. Map current grants into
it before proposing schema or UI changes. Keep product operations distinct from
external-action handoffs, and keep both distinct from the person's unrestricted
external conversation with their AI.

## Constraints

- Moving controls only its own tools, data, record operations and handoffs. It
  must not claim to control what a person discusses with their AI elsewhere.
- A Queue item or instruction never silently expands actor, scope, data,
  operation or external-action authority.
- Missing authority asks the smallest exact **Needs you** question; authority
  already granted must not create a redundant approval loop.
- Moving never independently contacts people, makes offers, purchases, books,
  signs, pays, publishes or takes another outside-world action.
- Sensitive data stays private by default and crosses only under an explicit
  actor, scope, data and operation/handoff grant.
- Do not claim compatibility, connection or action capability for every AI or
  provider. Label each path Current, Partial, Later or Unknown from proof.
- Schema, tool, Queue, settings, provider, audit and application changes use a
  normal branch, PR, full CI, migration/rollback review and deployment proof.

## Completion evidence

- One tested contract represents actor/AI, workspace/move/record scope,
  permitted fields/files/sensitive categories, product operation or
  external-action category and intent, approval, expiry/revocation and status.
- Every Moving tool and data request denies disallowed operations at the
  product boundary without language that polices external AI conversations.
- Marked-fixture Queue scenarios prove directive-without-grant → **Needs you**
  and prior-valid-grant → recorded handoff without silent authority expansion.
- Every handoff records grant, actor, scope, approved data/intent, approval and
  time, then attaches the attributable result, failure or return.
- Revocation and expiry prevent later tool/data/handoff use; read, create,
  update, delete, publish, share, export and external-action categories do not
  imply one another.
- Owner-facing and AI-facing truth surfaces list only verified compatible
  clients and capabilities with Current, Partial, Later or Unknown labels.
- Full CI, migration and rollback checks, normal deployment, marked-account
  browser/tool proof, retention/privacy review and independent audit pass.

## Unknown or access limitation

This philosophy-only audit used the existing dated capability evidence and
relevant tracker truth. It did not inspect production grants, private move data,
provider consoles, authenticated Queue records or external AI action results.
Exact current-path coverage and migration cost remain unknown.

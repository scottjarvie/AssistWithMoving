## Why this exists

OAuth can prove who signed in without proving what one AI may do inside Moving.
A person needs a visible, enforceable grant for the exact move context, private
evidence, writes, Queue work, and reversible archive actions they approve—and a
revoke control that stops the next discovery and tool call.

## Current truth

**Corrected 2026-08-17.** This section described the state before the work in the
History entry below landed, and its central claim — "There is no owner-visible
OAuth grant record or screen" — is no longer true.

Moving has scoped and revocable API keys, verified OAuth identity/client ids,
move access checks, Queue delegation, private-photo rules, audit fields, and
token/client cleanup receipts. On top of those, the product grant exists:
`convex/lib/aiGrants.ts` defines five separate scopes with published
does-not-imply boundaries; the `aiGrants` record binds one OAuth client to
selected moves, chosen scopes, an approval, a consent snapshot frozen at approval
time, an expiry, last use and revocation; `aiGrantActivities` keeps an
append-only owner-readable trail of uses and refusals; and `/settings/ai` is the
person-facing screen for all of it. The grant block is deployed — the live
branded `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp` document serves
`productGrantRequired`, the five `moving.*` scopes, `grantBoundaryVersion`
`2026-08-16`, and the grant manager URL.

Clerk still advertises only `openid profile email`, and that stays fine by
design: those are identity scopes and Moving never reads authority from a token
scope. `convex/lib/mcpGrantAccess.ts` re-reads the grant from the database on
every discovery and every tool call, which is exactly why offline JWT
verification is not a problem — revoking a connection refuses the very next call
while the access token is still cryptographically valid.

The broader external-action handoff requirement previously carried by this Card
is preserved separately in `MOV-0036`. `MOV-WO-010` is limited to Moving's own
tools, data, records, Queue operations, and reversible archive boundary.

## Next safe action

The mapping this Card called for is done. The canonical catalog

## Constraints

- Moving controls only its own tools, data, records, Queue, and handoffs. It
  does not control what a person discusses with their AI elsewhere.
- OAuth identity scopes never imply `moving.context.read`,
  `moving.evidence.read`, `moving.work.write`, `moving.queue.work`, or
  `moving.archive`.
- A Queue instruction never expands client, person, move, data, or operation
  authority. Missing authority returns the smallest exact **Needs you** action.
- Read, private-evidence read, write, Queue work, reversible archive, export,
  sharing/publishing, member management, and permanent delete stay separate.
- Derive person, household, and move access server-side. Never accept an
  arbitrary owner, tenant, or workspace selector from an AI.
- Preserve prior attribution after revocation. Do not erase history merely to
  make a client lose future access.
- Provider/account/security-policy changes, production data, secrets, billing,
  DNS, and irreversible actions require their own evidence and authority.

## Completion evidence

- A tested grant binds verified person, OAuth client, registration method,
  selected move(s), allowed scopes/data, approval, expiry, last use, revocation,
  and human-readable activity.
- Discovery and every tool call enforce the current product grant in addition
  to token identity; scope escalation, expired grant, and revoked grant fail
  without leaking another owner or record.
- Marked-fixture scenarios prove read-only, evidence-read, work-write,
  Queue-work, and archive separation; omitted scopes deny the action.
- Directive-without-grant becomes **Needs you**; a prior valid grant avoids a
  redundant approval loop and records the resulting handoff.
- A person can review and revoke the OAuth connection; the next discovery and
  tool call fail while existing result provenance remains visible.
- Migration/rollback, retention/privacy review, focused and full tests,
  protected release proof, marked lifecycle cleanup, and independent audit are
  recorded separately.

## Unknown or access limitation

The original alignment pass inspected source, tracker, philosophy, and live
anonymous discovery only. It did not inspect private production grants, change
Clerk, register a client, issue a token, or use a production move. The

grant block, and changed nothing about that limitation: no private production
grant has been inspected and no real client has held one.

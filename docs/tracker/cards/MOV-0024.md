## Why this exists

A move handoff must survive refreshes, abandoned AI runs, failures, account
exports, and later questions about who did what. A status badge without history
or recovery is not trustworthy Queue behavior.

## Current truth

Production source now provides append-only Queue activity, a broader audit event for
every transition, 15-minute claims, a bounded five-minute maintenance sweep,
retry budgets, explicit cancellation/expiry inside Done, exact Needs You
actions, readable Done results, indexed pagination/search, account export, and
an explicit anonymization/retention statement. Unit and in-memory integration
tests cover the complete person/AI lifecycle, replay, stale version, retry,
unauthorized claim and replay, cross-move result rejection, strict result and
retry bounds, and permanent move-purge coverage for Queue records. The
maintenance range explicitly excludes missing optional expiries, preventing
non-expiring items from being closed by the cron, and a reclaim records the
expired lease before starting the new claim. Mutations reject expired claim
leases and expired handoffs before accepting a late result. Indexed list pages
use opaque cursors and the lease-aware effective state, so expired stored
Working claims appear only under Waiting for your AI. Account-export queries
select Queue items and activity once by the exporting person's owner id,
independent of household membership, so move-only continuity is included
without exposing another person's Queue.
Expired claims use a maintenance-only release path rather than the live-lease
guard used for agents. Reclaim preserves the person's latest requested input,
terminal transitions clear expiry clocks, and durable result references validate
the table encoded by their declared type before move ownership is accepted.
Every person-provided answer is retained in immutable private Queue activity,
so repeated Needs You cycles do not overwrite their earlier provenance.

An authenticated Vercel-preview acceptance pass then created two clearly marked
synthetic moves and one one-day, move-restricted Queue key. The canonical item
completed Waiting for your AI → Working → Needs You → Waiting for your AI →
Working → Done with the exact human answer, readable result, and complete actor
activity visible in the browser. The REST boundary proved opaque continuation,
a stale-version `409`, and no cross-move exposure. The cross-move denial itself
was correct but surfaced as `400`; protected repair PR `#179` maps that authority
failure to `403`. The pass also found that the E2E cleanup helper omitted all
three Queue tables; `#179` adds them with regression coverage. Both synthetic
moves were permanently purged, the temporary key became invalid, and no real
household or move data was read.

The released Queue lifecycle scope is complete. The remaining live cron,
large-pagination, export-artifact, and second-identity scenarios are useful
future depth checks, not a reason to keep the foundation in Doing.

## Constraints

- Queue activity remains private move data; this package must not expose it via
  existing share links.
- Account deletion follows Moving's current shared-record anonymization policy;
  do not promise hard deletion that the product does not perform.
- Bounded queries are mandatory; no full-table Queue fetch/filter path.
- Do not backfill production capture rows without a read-only census, rollback,
  and explicit migration review.

## Completion evidence

- Claim expiry, configured expiry, release, cancel, retry available, retry
  exhausted, stale-version conflict, and exact idempotent replay pass in CI and a
  safe deployed fixture.
- Account export includes the exporting person's fixture item/activity, excludes
  another household member's Queue records, and share-link reads cannot retrieve it.
- Search and pagination remain bounded with more than one page of fixtures.
- Provider deployment and independent audit are linked.
- [x] PR `#179` locked complete Queue purge and semantic cross-move denial;
  later production Queue/MCP proof removed every marked Queue and move record.

## Why this exists

A green required workflow is useful only when maintainers can reproduce its
meaning locally and distinguish product regressions from a slow or flaky test
harness. Future retrofit work needs a trustworthy baseline.

## Current truth

At current main before tracker publication, lint completed with one warning and
typecheck passed. The local full Vitest run completed with 982 passing and 9
failing tests across six dense component files. Eight failures were five- or
45-second timeouts; one load-planner assertion could not find the expected
Movable units table after filtering. The last normal main GitHub run
`31272562770` passed required CI and its informational full suite at software
SHA `d6eb556ccc9ef2a4db88c59119460e27627b975c`. State-only commits after that
correctly skipped the full suite.

**Priority:** Next. **Change class:** verification/operating gap; any source or
test repair is software.

## Next safe action

Re-run the nine cases isolated and in the full suite under recorded machine and
worker settings, identify deterministic defects versus contention/timeouts,
then fix the product assertion or test architecture without simply raising
timeouts as a blanket response.

## Constraints

- Do not call a timeout a product failure without an independent reproduction.
- Do not hide a genuine missing-table or interaction defect by retrying it away.
- Preserve accessibility and user-event semantics in component tests.
- Test/config/source changes require a normal PR and full workflow.

## Completion evidence

- Each of the nine failures has a reproducible diagnosis, with before/after
  duration and whether it was product, assertion, or test-runtime contention.
- The full suite passes repeatedly under CI-equivalent settings and one
  resource-constrained local setting; no unexplained retries are required.
- The Movable units filter assertion is independently verified in a browser or
  component harness at the correct semantic layer.
- Required CI and the informational suite are linked for the exact fix SHA.

## Unknown or access limitation

This audit ran the suite once on one shared local machine. CI logs prove the
earlier software SHA passed, but they do not identify whether current local
failures are load-sensitive, machine-specific, or newly deterministic.
